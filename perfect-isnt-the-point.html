Perfect Isn't the Point

November 8, 2026

Somewhere along the way, a lot of people picked up the idea that art has to be perfect. Clean lines. No mistakes. Every piece better than the last. If it isn't flawless, it isn't worth showing, and maybe it isn't worth making at all.

That idea has stopped more artists than anything else ever will.

Art doesn't have to be perfect. It was never supposed to be. Art is expression. It's a feeling you're having right now, pulled out of your head and put somewhere other people can see it, hear it, or wear it. That's the whole job. Perfect has nothing to do with it.

Made from a feeling

Think about why you make anything in the first place. It's rarely because you sat down and decided to create something flawless. It's because something moved you. You were angry, or happy, or restless, or thinking about somebody, or you saw something that stuck with you and you couldn't shake it.

That feeling is the fuel. It's real, it's yours, and it only exists in that moment. Tomorrow you'll feel different. Next week you'll be somewhere else entirely. The piece you make today is a snapshot of who you were and what you felt when you made it.

When you chase perfect, you start to lose that. You overthink it. You sand down the rough edges until there's nothing left that sounds like you. You end up with something technically clean and emotionally empty.

Feeling over perfect. Every time.

A piece with a few mistakes and a lot of feeling will hit harder than a perfect piece with nothing behind it.

Imperfection is all over hip hop

Look at where this culture came from. Hip hop was built by people who didn't have the right equipment, the right training, or anybody's permission. They made it anyway, with whatever they had.

Graffiti has drips, overspray, and lines that wobbled because somebody was painting fast in bad light. Those aren't flaws. They're proof a person was there.

DJing came from taking records and doing things with them nobody was supposed to do. Scratching, cutting, looping a break over and over. It wasn't about playing the record correctly. It was about making it say something new.

MCs freestyle and stumble over a word, then flip the stumble into the next bar. The crowd remembers that moment more than a perfect verse.

Breaking is improvisation. Dancers react to the music in the moment, and sometimes a move doesn't land the way they planned. They keep going. That's the cypher.

The raw parts are what make it real. If hip hop had waited for perfect, it never would have happened.

Create your thoughts

You have ideas and feelings bouncing around in your head all the time. Most of them never go anywhere because they never get made. They stay thoughts.

Give them a shape. Draw them. Write them. Mix them. Paint them. Move to them. Put them on a shirt. It doesn't matter if the first version is rough. A rough version that exists beats a perfect version that only lives in your head.

You can't improve something you never made. You can't learn from a piece you were too nervous to start. Every artist you look up to has a pile of rough, awkward, unfinished work behind them. The only difference is they kept making things.

Don't let it discourage you

There will be times you finish something and it doesn't match what you pictured. The colors are off. The flow is clunky. It looks nothing like the image you had in your mind.

That feeling of disappointment is normal, and it's okay to feel it. Just don't let it convince you to stop.

The gap between what you imagine and what you can make right now is where all the growth happens. Every piece closes that gap a little more. If you quit because one piece didn't come out right, you never get to see how good you could have gotten.

Also remember that you're your own toughest critic. The flaws you can't stop staring at are usually things nobody else even notices. What they notice is the feeling. They notice that it came from somewhere real.

Appreciate the time you spent

This might be the most important part.

When you create something, you spent time doing what you love. You sat with your thoughts. You turned a feeling into something real. You made something that didn't exist before. That time matters, whether the final piece is a masterpiece or something you'll never show anyone.

Too many people only measure their art by the result. Was it good? Did people like it? Did it come out right? But the hours you spent making it were worth something on their own. That was time you were focused, present, and doing something for yourself. Not a lot of things in life give you that.

So next time you finish a piece, before you start picking it apart, take a second. Appreciate that you made it. Appreciate the time you put in. Appreciate that you had something to say and you said it.

Then go make the next one.
